Why the login wall, not the tutorial. Tutorial drop-off (29%) is the bigger raw number and the obvious place to look. But it's in line with genre norms for a 4-minute tutorial. The login step loses half its players against a genre benchmark closer to 20%. That gap, not the biggest bar, is the actual finding.
What's actually causing it

Two explanations fit a drop this size: the account-link flow is broken, or players don't want to link an account yet. Crash logs and review text point to only one of them.

Technical failure

  • No error-rate spike logged at this step
  • OAuth completion rate is 94% once started
  • No device or OS clustering in the drop
Not this. The flow itself works.

Trust and timing

  • App-store reviews: "why do I need an account to play a tutorial"
  • "forced login before I even know if I like the game"
  • Players quit at the exact screen the prompt appears, not gradually after
This is it. The ask comes before the payoff.

The trust story gets stronger when you split by how the player arrived. A broken flow would fail everyone at roughly the same rate. A trust problem wouldn't.

Paid UA traffic is colder by definition, arrives with less intent, and converts at nearly half the organic rate at this exact step. That's the signature of a trust ask landing on someone who hasn't decided they like the game yet, not a technical fault that should hit both groups evenly. It also means the fix matters most where the acquisition budget is actually spent.

The fix, screen by screen

Not "add a delay." The prompt has to move to a moment the player has something to protect, and it has to ask for less than it does today.

Why this specific trigger. The third screen doesn't fire on a timer. It fires the first time the player has something worth losing, a rare pull, a purchase, or a session over ten minutes, whichever comes first. A timer asks regardless of investment and reproduces the same problem on a delay.

Why one-tap sign-in, not email or OTP. OAuth completion is already 94% once started, so the current form isn't broken. But it's still the highest-friction option on the table. One-tap platform sign-in removes the form entirely, which matters most for exactly the paid UA segment that's least willing to invest effort right now.

Guest players aren't abandoned. A player who taps "Not now" sees the prompt again at most twice more, tied to future loss-aversion moments, then it goes quiet. A permanent, low-key "Save your progress" entry stays in the profile tab for whenever they're ready. No repeated nagging, no dead end.

How I'd ship this
01

Device-level 50/50 split on new installs

Randomize at the device, not the session, so a player never sees both versions. Run new installs only, no back-population of existing accounts.

02

Two-week read, guardrails checked daily

The expected jump, roughly 33% to 50%, is a large effect for a conversion metric, so it doesn't need a long run to reach significance. Two weeks is for the guardrails, not the primary metric.

03

10% soft launch before the full split

Ship to a small slice first to catch anything the funnel analysis couldn't predict, like a spike in uninstalls before a player ever links, before committing half of new installs to it.
